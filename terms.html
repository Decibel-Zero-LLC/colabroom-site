What the app tells you

Reading a recording

When you ask, CoLabRoom reads a recording to work out its key, tempo, chords, beats, sections, tune and words. What it works out is a first pass from what it heard, not a guarantee. It can be wrong, so check it against your own ears, and correct it in the app. Nothing it shows is a promise that a song is yours, original or free to use.

Your voice

When CoLabRoom analyses a recording, it works out the music and the words. It never uses your voice to recognise who you are, and never makes a voiceprint. We will not start doing so without asking you first, in writing, separately from these terms.

Clef

Clef is an AI guide, not a person. It answers what you ask about a song in the song's Ask box (our Privacy Policy calls this asking the app about a song). Its answers are written by an AI model, from Anthropic or OpenAI, from what the app has measured in your song; it never hears the recording. It can be wrong, and nothing it says is professional, medical or legal advice. Clef never writes your song for you: no words, melodies, parts or chords. We keep each question you ask Clef and its answer on your account, where only you can read them. You can't take one back yet, but they go when the song or your account is deleted. You can ask Clef 30 questions an hour. Clef isn't on in lesson rooms, and for now it is for people 18 and over.

Split drafts and music releases

A room can show a draft of who wrote a song and how the shares might divide, and CoLabRoom can print it, and print a music release filled in from your room's record. They are forms for you and the other musicians. The shares on a split draft are what the room's owner or an editor typed. The law usually treats people who write a song together as equal owners unless they agree otherwise in writing, so each of you should check it, and you may want your own lawyer or your songwriter society to look at it. These forms are not agreements with us, not a registration of ownership, and not legal advice. We are not a party to them, we sign nothing, and we have not checked them. What they say is what your room recorded and what you typed. Agree your splits with each other, and register them with your songwriter society yourselves. Printing a music release is for people 18 and over, and everyone on the song must be 18 or over too.

The free tools on colabroom.com

The chord finder and key finder read a recording you choose and give back a first guess at its chords or its key. The transpose, capo and numbers pages work in your browser. Use the tools only with recordings you made or have the right to use. We read the recording, send back the answer and let it go. We don't keep it, and nothing you send is used to train any model. The answer can be wrong. Together, the chord finder and key finder take 25 songs a day from each internet address. The tools are free, they can change or stop, and they are not meant for children under 13. What the site counts, and the one code it may remember in your browser, are described in our Privacy Policy.

The Open Mic, and your public page

The Open Mic is where you can list yourself, put a song where other musicians can hear it, and ask somebody you have never met to play on it. The Open Mic puts people in an order: a song that asks for a part you play comes first, then people you have played with, then people who share your sound, then your town, and every fourth card is somebody picked at random from those you may meet. It never scores anybody. Only put up music that you, and everybody on it, have the right to share, and never a cover.
